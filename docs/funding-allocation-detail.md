# Detailed Funding Allocation: Vitora and TibaBot

## Purpose and investment logic

This document explains how the proposed **US$250,000** funding requirement would be deployed to establish the operating foundation, commercial traction, customer delivery capability, clinical governance, and market evidence needed to grow Vitora and launch TibaBot responsibly.

Vitora is Nexora Consulting Ltd's hospital management information system (HMIS) for healthcare facilities. TibaBot is the patient-access and telemedicine product that will connect patients, clinicians, specialists, facilities, and, where suitable, payment or reimbursement workflows. The allocation deliberately funds both products where they share infrastructure, commercial channels, clinical networks, and payer relationships, while preserving specific investment for their distinct delivery needs.

The budget is an execution envelope rather than a guarantee that every line item will be spent in equal monthly amounts. Funds should be released against approved workplans, vendor quotations or contracts where applicable, and evidence of progress. Material movement between categories should require management approval, with particular protection for security, clinical quality, legal/compliance, and customer implementation commitments.

## 1. Office Setup & Operating Infrastructure - $15,000 (6.0%)

### Why this allocation matters

Vitora and TibaBot require a professional operating environment from which staff can sell, implement, train, support, and demonstrate healthcare products. A modest but capable office improves customer confidence, enables hands-on deployment activities, and provides a secure setting for internal collaboration and customer training.

### Detailed use of funds

- Office rent and deposit, utilities, and initial premises setup.
- Workstations, desks, chairs, secure storage, and a small meeting space.
- A demonstration and training room for facility teams, remote demonstrations, and onboarding sessions.
- Computers, monitors, printers/scanners, networking equipment, and reliable internet installation.
- Backup internet, UPS or other power-backup equipment, and basic physical office security.
- External and internal signage, office supplies, and other essential operating consumables.

### Expected outputs and controls

The intended output is a ready-to-use office capable of supporting sales demonstrations, staff training, customer meetings, and secure day-to-day operations. Procurement should favour durable equipment, documented asset ownership, warranties, and right-sized recurring commitments. Office spend should remain operationally necessary and avoid excessive fit-out or non-essential furnishings.

## 2. Cloud, Security & Infrastructure - $22,000 (8.8%)

### Why this allocation matters

Healthcare software depends on service availability, data protection, recoverability, and traceability. This allocation supports the underlying technology environment for both Vitora and TibaBot, including production operations, engineering environments, and the security controls expected by facilities, clinicians, payers, and regulators.

### Detailed use of funds

- Azure hosting, compute, managed databases, storage, and network services for Vitora and TibaBot.
- Production, development, staging, and demonstration environments that permit controlled testing before release.
- Automated backups, restoration testing, disaster-recovery infrastructure, and capacity for critical incident recovery.
- Monitoring, logging, alerting, domain/DNS management, TLS certificates, and transactional email or service infrastructure.
- CI/CD systems, code-quality tooling, release automation, and environment configuration controls.
- Security monitoring, endpoint security, vulnerability scanning, penetration testing, and access-control tooling.
- Encryption and key-management controls, incident-response preparation, and an infrastructure scaling reserve for verified demand.

### Expected outputs and controls

The target outcome is a monitored and recoverable service environment with documented backups, access controls, audit trails, security remediation practices, and deployment procedures. Spend should be governed through cloud budgets, role-based access, monthly consumption review, alert thresholds, and security findings tracked to remediation. The scaling reserve should be used only when usage, customer onboarding, or resilience requirements demonstrate the need.

Vitora's commercial success depends on converting healthcare facilities from awareness to contracted, active customers. This allocation funds the people, tools, travel, and sales materials required to develop a disciplined pipeline rather than relying on opportunistic selling.

### Expected outputs and controls

The expected outputs are a qualified target-account list, an active CRM pipeline, repeatable discovery and demonstration materials, proposals, commercial terms, and early reference accounts. Core indicators include qualified leads, demonstrations completed, proposal-to-contract conversion, sales cycle duration, contracted annual value, and customer acquisition cost. Commissions should be tied to defined milestones, such as signed contracts and verified collections, rather than unqualified leads alone.

## 4. Vitora Implementation, Migration & Customer Success - $28,000 (11.2%)

### Why this allocation matters

For an HMIS, sale completion is not the same as customer value. Facilities need a structured transition from legacy processes and data into live clinical, operational, billing, pharmacy, and laboratory workflows. This allocation reduces go-live risk and makes customer retention a deliberate operating function.

### Detailed use of funds

- Pre-go-live discovery, facility workflow mapping, implementation plans, and readiness assessments.
- Data-cleaning support and migration of approved legacy-HMIS data, with verification before production use.
- System configuration, user provisioning, role and permission configuration, and facility-specific workflow setup.
- Billing, pharmacy, laboratory, and relevant integration configuration based on each facility's licensed modules and readiness.
- Onsite or remote deployment, staff training, super-user training, go-live support, and implementation documentation.
- Post-go-live support, issue escalation, adoption monitoring, customer-success management, retention, and renewal support.

### Expected outputs and controls

Each implementation should produce an approved implementation plan, configured tenant environment, named super-users, training records, migration validation evidence where migration is in scope, go-live checklist, support contacts, and post-go-live review. Success indicators include time to go-live, trained-user completion, adoption of agreed workflows, migration exceptions resolved, support response time, customer health, retention, and renewal rate. Data migration must use authorised extracts, documented mapping, access controls, validation, and secure disposal or return procedures.

## Financial model

### Source, scope, and conversion basis

This section summarises the reconciled financial assumptions and projections in `vitora-marketing/public/financial-model.html`. The model covers financial years **2026-2030** and is a five-year Vitora HMIS planning model. It is not a forecast guarantee, audited statement, or a claim that the US$250,000 allocation funds the full five-year scale case.

The source presents monetary amounts in Kenya shillings (KES). Every monetary figure below has been converted using the fixed rate requested for this document:

`US$ = KES / 130`

Dollar figures are rounded to the nearest dollar below US$1 million and to two decimal places above US$1 million. Percentages, facility counts, years, customer counts, multiples, and durations are unchanged. The conversion does not change the underlying commercial, operating, or market assumptions.

The non-payroll schedule is driven by operating activity rather than broad percentage growth:

- **Cloud and security:** Clinics and hospitals share a multi-tenant platform: KES 80,000 monthly baseline plus KES 100 per average active shared tenant per month. Enterprise customers alone receive a dedicated environment at KES 10,000 per average enterprise tenant per month. Periodic penetration testing and security assurance remain funded through the legal, compliance, and governance reserve rather than inflating recurring hosting cost.

These targets must be tracked separately from commercial overhead and reviewed against observed acquisition cohorts.
- **Implementation:** Clinics and hospitals are tenant onboarding activities, not separate deployments: KES 5,000 per clinic and KES 15,000 per hospital. Enterprise customers alone receive dedicated deployment at KES 120,000 each. A shared-platform release and enablement reserve scales from KES 0.5M to KES 1.6M annually. This requires standardised remote onboarding and limited complex migration work; exceptional onsite or migration requirements must be separately approved.
- **Office and administration:** The plan assumes a hybrid operation beginning at KES 100,000 per month in Year 1, rather than a conventional office footprint.
- **Legal, governance, and TibaBot delivery:** These are explicit reserves. TibaBot remains a separate clinical-service contribution model, not an assumed 88% gross-margin SaaS product.

### Breakeven

The annual model reaches positive EBITDA in **Year 3**, when revenue is projected at approximately **$374,981** and EBITDA at approximately **$78,827**. This is an annual planning result, not a claim that the business will have positive cash in every month of Year 3.

The Year 3 breakeven outcome depends on all of the following remaining true:

- The KES 100,000 maximum all-in monthly FTE cost is maintained.
- Clinics and hospitals remain on the shared multi-tenant platform rather than requiring dedicated environments.
- Enterprise customers alone incur dedicated deployment and environment costs.
- Customer acquisition, onboarding, cloud usage, and support costs stay within the stated driver assumptions.
- Implementation remains standardised, with complex migrations, onsite work, and specialist contractors separately approved.
- TibaBot clinical delivery remains within its explicit reserve and is not treated as a high-margin SaaS revenue stream.

Before presenting a firm break-even date externally, management should translate this annual model into a monthly cash-flow model that includes invoice timing, collections, annual-billing discounts, implementation timing, payroll dates, statutory payments, tax, and capital expenditure. The practical decision point is therefore: **Year 3 EBITDA breakeven is the base-case target; monthly cash breakeven must be validated separately.**

### Sensitivity boundaries

## Portfolio-level governance and reporting

The allocation should be managed as one integrated execution portfolio. Monthly reporting should show budget committed, budget spent, remaining funds, material variance, cash runway implications, completed milestones, risks, and decisions needed. A practical reporting dashboard can group progress under the following dimensions:

- **Commercial traction:** qualified opportunities, demonstrations, proposals, contracts, collections, and reference customers.
- **Customer delivery:** implementation status, time to go-live, trained users, adoption indicators, support responsiveness, customer health, and renewal outlook.
- **Product and infrastructure:** release milestones, uptime, incidents, backup and restoration checks, security findings, and remediation status.
- **Clinical operations:** verified clinicians, service availability, escalation compliance, incidents, audit completion, and patient or clinician experience.
- **Partnerships and expansion:** payer, association, academic, and regional-market milestones; pilot status; and go/no-go decisions.
- **Evidence and compliance:** research progress, documented findings, contracts, privacy actions, regulatory advice, and outstanding risk items.

Management should maintain a decision log for material changes, protect patient and customer data in all implementation and research activity, and ensure that commercial pressure does not override clinical safety, privacy, security, or regulatory obligations. This discipline ensures that the funding supports not only initial growth, but also the trustworthy operational capability required in healthcare.
